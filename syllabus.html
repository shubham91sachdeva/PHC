Personal Hygiene

Hands, body, clothes, hair — building daily clean habits

tap to expand
Learning Objectives

Understand why we wash hands and when we must

Know that bathing daily keeps illness away

Recognise that clean clothes, nails, and hair are part of health

Be able to perform the 6-step handwashing routine independently

Content
  • Handwashing: The 6-step WHO method — palm-to-palm, between fingers, backs of hands, thumbs, fingertips, wrists. When to wash: before eating, after toilet, after playing outside, after touching animals, after coughing/sneezing
  • Soap science (simple): Germs are tiny creatures that make us sick. Soap lifts them off our skin. Water alone is not enough.
  • Bathing routine: Why daily bathing matters. Body parts that need extra attention — armpits, feet, behind ears. Drying fully after bath.
  • Nail care: Trimming nails short, cleaning under nails, what lives under dirty nails (germs, parasites)
  • Hair care: Regular washing, combing, lice prevention and recognition — no shame attached
  • Clothes hygiene: Wearing clean clothes daily, changing undergarments, wearing socks with shoes
  • Sneezing/coughing etiquette: Into the elbow, not hands. Disposing tissues. Washing hands after.
  • Toilet hygiene: Proper wiping, flushing, always washing hands with soap after toilet
Activities
Activity 1

Germ Glitter: Coat hands in glitter = germs. Try to remove with water only. Then soap. See the difference. Unforgettable.

Activity 2

Hygiene Song: Set the 6-step handwash routine to a 20-second tune children can memorise and sing while washing.

Activity 3

Clean Body Chart: Students draw themselves and label 5 body parts they clean every day and how.

Activity 4

Hygiene Pledge Board: Class creates a "We Wash Our Hands" pledge board with their handprints in paint.

Dental Health

Teeth, brushing, food choices, and visiting the dentist

tap to expand
Learning Objectives

Know how to brush teeth correctly (2 minutes, twice daily)

Understand that sugar feeds the germs that make holes in teeth

Know that milk teeth fall out and adult teeth are permanent

Not be afraid of the dentist — see checkups as normal and good

Content
  • Types of teeth: Incisors (cutting), canines (tearing), molars (grinding). Milk teeth vs adult teeth — why milk teeth still matter
  • Brushing technique: Soft circular motion on all surfaces — front, back, top. 2 minutes. Twice a day — morning and before bed. Why before bed is more important.
  • Toothbrush care: Rinse after use, store upright, replace every 3 months (or when bristles splay)
  • Tongue cleaning: Bacteria live on the tongue too. A tongue scraper or back of toothbrush cleans it.
  • Sugar and cavities: Sugar + mouth bacteria = acid = holes in teeth (cavities). The simpler the explanation, the better remembered.
  • Foods that help teeth: Milk, curd, paneer, cheese (calcium), crunchy vegetables (natural scrubbing), water (washes away sugar)
  • Foods that harm teeth: Toffees, chewing gum, biscuits that stick, cold drinks, fruit juice (even fresh — acidic)
  • Dentist visits: What a dentist does, what tools they use, why it doesn't have to be scary. Annual dental camp held at school.
  • Fluoride toothpaste: Why it matters — strengthens tooth enamel. Children should use a pea-sized amount.
Activities
Activity 1

Egg in Vinegar Experiment: Soak an egg in vinegar (= acid from sugar on teeth). Watch enamel dissolve over 24 hours. Powerful visual demonstration.

Activity 2

Brush-Along: Toothbrush brought from home (or provided). 2-minute guided brushing session with timer and music.

Activity 3

Smile Chart: Students draw their smile. Mark which teeth are milk teeth and which are adult teeth (or expected to be).

Activity 4

Good Food / Bad Food Sort: Picture cards of foods sorted into "teeth friends" and "teeth enemies."

Nutrition Basics

Colours on the plate, eating variety, drinking water

tap to expand
Content
  • The Rainbow Plate: Eating different coloured foods gives different powers — red (lycopene/immune), orange (Vitamin A/eyes), green (iron/strength), yellow (Vitamin C), white (calcium)
  • Food groups simply: Grains (energy), Dal/Eggs/Milk (building), Vegetables/Fruits (protection), Fats & Oils (small amounts). A balanced meal has all.
  • Why breakfast matters: The brain and body need fuel after sleep. Skipping breakfast = poor concentration. Simple story-based explanation.
  • Water: Body is 60% water. We need 6–8 glasses a day. Thirst means we are already a little dehydrated. Water over cold drinks always.
  • Junk food awareness (gentle): Chips, biscuits, cold drinks — fine sometimes, not every day. What does "sometimes food" mean?
  • Indian traditional foods: Dal-roti, khichdi, curd-rice — why these are actually very healthy and have been eaten for generations
  • Eating slowly: The stomach takes 20 minutes to tell the brain it's full. Chewing well = better digestion
Activities
Activity 1

Rainbow Plate Art: Students draw and colour a plate with at least 5 different coloured foods and label each.

Activity 2

My Breakfast Show & Tell: Students describe (or draw) what they ate for breakfast and the class identifies which food groups are present.

Movement & Play

Why bodies need to move — fun, fitness, and outdoor play

tap to expand
Content
  • Bodies need movement: Just like plants need sunlight, bodies need movement to grow strong. 60 minutes of active play every day.
  • Types of play: Running, jumping, climbing, swimming, dancing, cycling, traditional Indian games (kabaddi, kho-kho, lagori, gilli-danda)
  • Why screens are different from play: Screens rest the body; play builds it. The difference between active and passive time.

  • Warming up and cooling down: Why we stretch before running. What a pulled muscle feels like and why we should prevent it.
  • Sports and games: All movement counts. There is no "bad at sports" — every body can move in some way.

Body parts, organs, how the body works — basic anatomy

tap to expand
Content

Safety & Good Touch Bad Touch

Body autonomy, personal safety, trusted adults

tap to expand
Delivery note: This topic is delivered with trained female and male facilitators in age-appropriate language. No graphic content. Role-play with puppets recommended for Class 1–2.
Content
  • Good touch: A hug from a family member who asks first, a high-five from a friend, a doctor examining you with a parent present
  • Bad touch: Any touch on private parts not for medical reasons, any touch that feels wrong even if from someone you know
  • Confusing touch: Sometimes someone familiar does something that feels wrong. It is still not okay. You are still allowed to say no.
  • Body belongs to you: No one has the right to touch your body without your permission — not a relative, teacher, or friend
  • Say No, Go, Tell: The three-step safety rule. Say no, get away, tell a trusted adult immediately
  • Trusted adults: Who are your 3 trusted adults? Parent, teacher, school counsellor, another relative. Practice saying their names.
  • Secrets vs surprises: Good surprises are temporary (a birthday gift). Bad secrets make you feel uncomfortable — these should always be told.
  • POCSO Act (simple): A law in India protects children. If someone hurts your body, they can go to jail. You will not get in trouble for telling.
Activities
Activity 1

Puppet Role-Play: Puppets act out scenarios. Children call out "Good Touch" or "Bad Touch" and decide what the puppet should do.

Activity 2

My Safety Circle: Students draw their 3 trusted adults and practice saying "I will tell [name] if I feel unsafe."

Emotions & Feelings

Naming feelings, expressing them safely, calming down

tap to expand
Content
  • All feelings are okay: Happy, sad, angry, scared, excited, embarrassed, proud — all feelings are normal. What matters is what we do with them.
  • Naming feelings: Using words to name emotions instead of acting out. "I feel angry because…" vs hitting or crying without explaining.
  • Feelings in the body: Butterflies in stomach = nervous. Tight chest = scared or sad. Racing heart = excited or scared. Learning to read body signals.
  • Calming down tools: Deep belly breaths (3 slow breaths), count to 10, squeeze a stress ball, draw how you feel, tell a trusted adult
  • Empathy: How does someone else feel? Noticing when a friend is sad or scared and what you can do to help.
  • Asking for help: It is brave to say "I need help." It is strong to tell someone when something is wrong.
Activities
Activity 1

Feelings Faces Wheel: Spinning wheel with 8 emotions. Students spin and make the face, then describe a time they felt that way.

Activity 2

Balloon Breath: Students learn belly breathing by placing hands on stomach and feeling it rise like a balloon. Practise daily for 2 minutes.

Personal Hygiene

Science of hygiene, puberty hygiene, disease prevention

tap to expand
Content
  • Microbiology basics: What bacteria and viruses are, how they spread — contact, droplet, faecal-oral routes. Why hand hygiene breaks the chain of infection.
  • The science of soap: Surfactants — how soap molecules surround and lift bacteria/viruses off the skin. Alcohol hand sanitiser vs soap.
  • Puberty hygiene changes: Increased sweat glands activated during puberty → body odour. Deodorant vs antiperspirant. Daily bathing becomes more important.
  • Acne and skin care: Sebaceous glands activated by androgens. Why pimples happen. Gentle cleansing — not scrubbing. Not popping pimples. When to see a dermatologist.
  • Menstrual hygiene (girls): Changing sanitary products every 4–6 hours regardless of flow. Washing hands before and after. Disposal in bins, not toilets.
  • Foot and nail hygiene: Fungal infections (athlete's foot, ringworm) — moisture, shared shoes, damp socks. Prevention and recognition.
  • Oral hygiene revisited: The link between oral bacteria and systemic disease. Flossing introduced. Mouthwash — when appropriate.
  • Toilet hygiene in school: Using school toilets safely — Pee Safe toilet seat sanitizer use, why squatting vs sitting matters for hygiene in shared facilities.
  • Shared items: Never share combs, razors, towels, water bottles — transmission of infection, lice, hepatitis B
Activities
Activity 1

Agar Plate Experiment: Swab hands before and after washing onto agar plates. Observe bacterial growth over 48 hours. Visual proof of handwashing effectiveness.

Activity 2

Hygiene Audit: Students audit their own daily hygiene routine against a checklist, identify gaps, and make a 1-week improvement plan.

Dental Health

Oral biology, diet and teeth, orthodontics, dental checkups

tap to expand
Content
  • Tooth structure: Enamel (hardest substance in body), dentine, pulp, root. What each layer does and what damages it.
  • Dental caries mechanism: Streptococcus mutans bacteria → ferments sugar → lactic acid → demineralises enamel → cavity. Understanding this makes the "avoid sugar" message logical, not arbitrary.
  • Plaque and tartar: What dental plaque is, how it hardens into tartar, why professional cleaning is needed.
  • Gum disease (gingivitis): Signs — bleeding gums, swollen gums. Cause: plaque at the gum line. Reversible with proper brushing and flossing.
  • Flossing technique: Proper flossing method introduced. Why toothbrush cannot reach between teeth.
  • Nutrition and dental health: Calcium (RDA 1000–1300mg for this age group), Vitamin D (required for calcium absorption), fluoride.
  • Diet timing: It is not just what you eat but when and how often. Sipping sweet drinks all day = constant acid attack.
  • Orthodontics: What braces are and why some children need them — not cosmetic but functional. How to maintain hygiene with braces.
  • Teeth grinding (bruxism): Often stress-related. Signs, consequences, when to seek help.
  • Disclosing tablets: Used to reveal plaque — students brush normally, then use tablet to see missed areas. Self-directed improvement.
Activities
Activity 1

Plaque Disclosure: Students brush, then use a disclosing tablet. Pink/red areas show missed plaque. Re-brush. Compare before/after. Transformative for brushing habits.

Activity 2

Label Reading — Dental Edition: Students read sugar content on popular snacks and drinks. Calculate daily sugar exposure. Compare to WHO recommended maximum (25g/day).

Activity 3

Annual Dental Camp: School-hosted dental checkup by PHC partner dentist. Every student gets a basic dental screening and a take-home report.

Activity 4

Flossing Practice: Each student gets a piece of dental floss. Step-by-step guided practice. Many will never have flossed before.

Nutrition Science

Macronutrients, micronutrients, BMI, gut health, reading labels

tap to expand
Content
  • Macronutrients: Proteins (amino acids — complete vs incomplete, animal vs plant), Carbohydrates (simple vs complex, glycaemic index basics), Fats (saturated, unsaturated, trans fats — which are which in Indian diet)
  • Protein in growth: Ages 10–14 need ~0.9g/kg/day. Why muscles, enzymes, hormones, and immunity all need protein. Best Indian sources: dal, paneer, soya, eggs, milk, sprouts.
  • Micronutrients critical for this age: Iron (especially girls — menstrual loss), Calcium (peak bone building years), Zinc (immunity, puberty), Iodine (thyroid, brain development), Vitamin D (bone + immunity — most Indian urban children are deficient)
  • BMI: Body Mass Index = weight(kg) / height(m)². Age-specific percentiles for children (not adult BMI). What it measures (body size ratio), what it doesn't measure (body composition).
  • Obesity awareness: India has 14.4 million obese children — second highest globally. Cause: ultra-processed food + sedentary behaviour + genetics. Not a moral failing. Health consequences: insulin resistance, joint stress, sleep apnea, mental health.
  • The Gut Microbiome: 100 trillion bacteria in the gut. Diversity = health. Fibre feeds good bacteria. Antibiotics kill good and bad bacteria. Probiotics (curd, buttermilk, idli, dosa) restore balance.
  • Hydration: Urine colour chart as a hydration guide. Coffee and tea dehydrate. Cold drinks have 10 teaspoons of sugar per 330ml can.
  • Reading nutrition labels: Per 100g vs per serving sleight of hand. Added sugar, saturated fat, sodium — what to look for.
  • Fad diets and myths: Keto, GM diet, starvation — why they don't work for growing children. "Detox" is not a real biological process.
  • Canteen audit: Students assess their school canteen offerings against nutritional guidelines and make suggestions for healthier options.
Activities
Activity 1

Protein Meter: Daily food log of protein intake vs target. Students track for one week and identify their protein gap. Use the PHC app tracker.

Activity 2

Design Your Thali: Using food cards, students build a balanced Indian thali that meets macronutrient and micronutrient targets for their age group.

Activity 3

Label Reading Lab: 10 popular packaged foods. Students read labels, identify hidden sugars, calculate per-portion sugar, and rank from healthiest to least healthy.

Physical Fitness

Fitness components, exercise types, home workouts, sedentary risk

tap to expand
Content
  • 5 components of fitness: Cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, body composition
  • Aerobic vs anaerobic: Aerobic (with oxygen — running, swimming, cycling). Anaerobic (without — sprinting, weight training). Both are needed.
  • Safe strength training for 10–14: Bodyweight exercises are safe and effective. Free weights safe after puberty with guidance. Machines safe with supervision. No concern about "stunting growth" — this is a myth.
  • Exercises at home (no equipment): Push-ups, squats, lunges, planks, jumping jacks, mountain climbers, glute bridges. Progressions for each.
  • Flexibility and mobility: Static vs dynamic stretching. When to stretch (after warm-up, after exercise — not cold muscles). Yoga asanas as flexibility training.
  • Sedentary behaviour risk: Sitting for more than 2 consecutive hours is an independent health risk even if you exercise. The importance of movement breaks.
  • Fitness assessment (baseline): Step test (cardiovascular), push-up count (upper body), flexibility test (sit-and-reach), BMI. Results are personal — not compared between students.
  • FITT principle: Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type — a simple framework for planning exercise
  • Injury prevention: Warm-up (5–10 min dynamic movement), cool-down (5 min static stretch), hydration, proper footwear, rest days
  • Sport as social health: Team sports build communication, cooperation, resilience. Individual sports build self-discipline. Both are valuable.

Puberty & Body Changes

Hormones, physical changes, emotional changes, body image

tap to expand
Delivery note: Boys and girls in separate groups for core content. Same-gender facilitator required. Emphasise: all bodies develop at different times — all timelines are normal.
Content
  • What is puberty: The body's transition from child to adult under direction of hormones. It is not a problem — it is biology.
  • Hormones: Oestrogen and progesterone (girls), testosterone (boys), GnRH (both) — what they are and what they trigger
  • Physical changes — girls: Breast development (Tanner stages), pubic/underarm hair, growth spurt, hip widening, body fat redistribution, onset of menstruation, vaginal discharge (normal)
  • Physical changes — boys: Testicular growth, penile growth, pubic/underarm/facial hair, voice breaking, muscle development, growth spurt, ejaculation/nocturnal emissions (normal and not shameful)
  • Changes in both: Acne, body odour, emotional volatility, increased sexual curiosity (normal)
  • Timeline: Girls typically begin puberty 8–13 years, boys 9–14 years. Completing puberty takes 2–5 years. Early and late developers — both completely normal.
  • Body image: What it is (how we see and feel about our bodies). The media's unrealistic standards. Body comparison and its mental health effects.
  • Body neutrality: Moving from "I hate my body" or "I love my body" to "My body does incredible things for me."
  • Height and weight changes: Growth spurts, appetite increases, sleep requirement increases — all normal. Not "greedy" or "lazy."

Menstrual Health & Hygiene

Cycle awareness, products, Pee Safe kits, common concerns

tap to expand
Delivery note: Girls-only session for detailed content. A brief, respectful awareness session is held with boys on menstruation — to build empathy and reduce stigma. Female facilitator required.
Content — Girls
  • The menstrual cycle: 4 phases — menstruation, follicular, ovulation, luteal. Average cycle 21–35 days. What happens hormonally in each phase.
  • Period basics: Duration 2–7 days. Blood volume 30–80ml per cycle. Brown/dark blood at start/end — normal. Clots — small clots normal; large clots see a doctor.
  • Sanitary products — full comparison:
    • Sanitary napkins (pads): Most common in India. Change every 4–6 hours. Dispose in bins with paper wrap. Never flush.
    • Tampons: Internal. Change every 4–8 hours max (Toxic Shock Syndrome risk if left longer). Require practice to insert.
    • Menstrual cups: Reusable, cost-effective, eco-friendly. 8–12 hours between emptying. 3–6 month learning curve but highly effective.
    • Period underwear: Absorbent underwear. Best as backup or for light days. Wash and reuse.
  • Menstrual hygiene: Change every 4–6 hours (even light flow — bacteria multiply in warm blood). Wash hands before and after handling products. Bathe daily during period — no restriction. Wear breathable cotton underwear.
  • Period pain (dysmenorrhea): Prostaglandins cause uterine contractions → cramps. Heat pad, hydration, light movement help. Ibuprofen/mefenamic acid can be used under parent/doctor guidance. Severe pain = see a doctor (possible endometriosis).
  • PMS: Premenstrual Syndrome — mood changes, bloating, breast tenderness, fatigue in the luteal phase. Caused by hormone fluctuations. Exercise, reduced salt/caffeine, adequate sleep help.
  • Irregular periods: Normal in the first 1–2 years after menarche. After that, consult a doctor for cycles under 21 or over 35 days, very heavy flow, or missed periods.
  • Period myths debunked: No restrictions on bathing, exercise, eating certain foods, or visiting religious places. None of these have any medical basis.
  • Period at school: What to do if your period starts unexpectedly. Spare pad in bag. How to ask a female teacher for help. Using the sick room.
🩷 Pee Safe School Sanitary Kit — PHC Programme

Every girl student from Class 6 onwards receives an annual Pee Safe PHC Sanitary Kit at the start of the school year containing: a 7-day supply of sanitary napkins, a hygiene pouch, a menstrual health information card, and a Pee Safe toilet seat sanitizer spray for use in school toilets. Pee Safe vending machines are installed in girls' toilets in schools with 200+ girl students. A Pee Safe representative conducts one annual in-school menstrual health awareness session. The programme removes the access barrier to menstrual hygiene products for all girls in PHC schools.

  • Buying sanitary products for a family member is a normal, helpful act

Sexual Safety & Body Rights

POCSO, consent basics, online safety, recognising unsafe situations

tap to expand
Content
  • Body autonomy: Your body belongs to you at every age. No adult, relative, friend, or authority figure has the right to touch you in a way that feels wrong.
  • POCSO Act 2012: Protection of Children from Sexual Offences. Any sexual act with a person under 18 is a crime in India regardless of consent. Children should know this law exists to protect them.
  • Consent: An enthusiastic, ongoing, and freely given "yes." Silence is not consent. Saying yes once doesn't mean yes forever. Consent can be withdrawn at any time.
  • Grooming awareness: How adults who intend harm gain a child's trust over time — gifts, secrecy, isolation from family. Recognising grooming patterns.
  • Online safety: Never share personal photos, location, school name, or home address with strangers online. Fake profiles are common. Screenshotting and sharing of private images is a crime (IT Act + POCSO).
  • What to do if something happens: Tell a trusted adult immediately. You will not be punished. The adult is responsible, not you. Keep telling until someone listens.
  • Helplines: Childline India — 1098 (free, 24/7). iCall — 9152987821.

Mental Health

Stress, anxiety awareness, sleep, help-seeking

tap to expand
Content
  • Mental health definition: "A state of wellbeing in which every individual realises their potential, can cope with normal stresses, can work productively, and is able to make a contribution to the community." (WHO) — not just absence of illness.
  • Stress physiology: HPA axis activation — hypothalamus → pituitary → adrenal glands → cortisol + adrenaline. Fight/flight/freeze. Good stress (eustress) vs harmful chronic stress (distress).
  • Exam stress: Moderate stress improves performance (Yerkes-Dodson curve). Too much stress impairs memory and concentration. Practical management: schedule, breaks, sleep.
  • Anxiety basics: Worry that is disproportionate to the threat. Physical symptoms (racing heart, sweating, tight chest). Different from normal nervousness.
  • Social anxiety: Fear of being judged or embarrassed in social situations. Very common at this age. Does not mean something is wrong with you.
  • Sleep and mental health: Sleep deprivation causes anxiety, irritability, poor concentration, low mood. 9–11 hours for ages 10–14. Blue light from screens suppresses melatonin — screen off 1 hour before bed.
  • Stress relief tools: 4-7-8 breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding, journaling, exercise, talking to someone, spending time in nature
  • When to get help: If stress/anxiety lasts more than 2 weeks, interferes with school, eating, or sleep, or feels overwhelming — tell a parent, teacher, or school counsellor.
  • Reducing stigma: Going to a psychologist is like going to a doctor for a fever. Mental illness is not weakness, laziness, or drama.

Emotional Health & EQ

Emotional vocabulary, self-regulation, empathy, resilience

tap to expand
Content
  • Emotional intelligence (EQ): Four domains — self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship management. Research shows EQ predicts life success better than IQ.
  • Expanding emotional vocabulary: Beyond happy/sad/angry — frustrated, overwhelmed, disappointed, proud, content, envious, embarrassed, humiliated, nostalgic. Naming accurately → managing better.
  • Emotional regulation: Pause before reacting. STOP technique: Stop, Take a breath, Observe what you're feeling, Proceed mindfully.
  • Emotional triggers: What makes you angry? What makes you anxious? Knowing your triggers = better self-management.
  • Resilience: The ability to recover from difficulty. Not about not feeling pain — about not being permanently broken by it. Growth mindset.
  • Gratitude practice: 3 things I am grateful for today. Research shows consistent gratitude practice measurably improves mental health within 4 weeks.
  • Self-compassion: Treating yourself with the same kindness you'd show a good friend when you make a mistake. Different from excusing bad behaviour.

Social Health

Friendships, peer pressure, bullying, boundaries

tap to expand
Content
  • Social health defined: The quality of your relationships and your ability to communicate, cooperate, and set limits with others.
  • Healthy vs toxic friendships: Healthy: mutual respect, honesty, support. Toxic: manipulation, jealousy, exclusion, making you feel bad about yourself.
  • Peer pressure mechanics: Direct (being asked to do something) vs indirect (feeling like you must do what others do to fit in). Assertiveness without aggression.
  • Assertiveness scripts: "I don't feel comfortable doing that." "That's not for me." "I'll think about it and let you know." — Practise saying these without apology.
  • Bullying: Physical, verbal, social/relational, cyber. Bystander responsibility. How to support a victim without making it worse. How to report safely.
  • Setting limits: Limits on time, touch, information you share. A limit is not rude — it is a communication of what you need.
  • Communication skills: Active listening (eye contact, reflecting back, not interrupting). "I" statements vs "you" statements in conflict.

Digital Wellness

Screen time, social media, dopamine, online safety

tap to expand
Content
  • Dopamine and screens: Social media is engineered to trigger dopamine (reward chemical). Variable reward schedules (like slot machines) create compulsive use. Understanding the design means you can use it intentionally.
  • Screen time and health: WHO recommends max 2 hours recreational screen time per day for ages 10–14. Effects of excess: sleep disruption, anxiety, reduced attention span, eye strain, posture problems.
  • Social comparison: Instagram/Reels show edited, curated highlights. Comparing your real life to others' highlight reel causes anxiety and low self-esteem. Awareness breaks the spell.
  • Digital footprint: Everything you post, like, and share creates a permanent record. Future employers and universities can see it. Digital hygiene = thinking before posting.
  • Online identity and safety: What to share vs not share (location, school, photos, home address). Stranger danger extends to the internet. Verification: most "teen" strangers online are not teens.
  • Cyberbullying: Anonymous cruelty is still cruelty. Taking screenshots as evidence. Blocking and reporting. Telling a trusted adult.
  • Gaming health: Gaming can be social, creative, and fun. Signs of problematic gaming: losing sleep, ignoring food, angry when asked to stop, losing interest in other activities.
  • Digital detox: A planned break from screens. Not punishment — a conscious reset. What can you do instead? A 24-hour challenge.

Advanced Hygiene & Infection Control

Immunology basics, communicable disease, hygiene in adult life

tap to expand
Content
  • Immune system basics: Innate vs adaptive immunity. Lymphocytes (T-cells, B-cells), antibodies, inflammation as a protective response. How vaccines work — herd immunity.
  • Communicable disease transmission: R0 (basic reproduction number) explained simply. How hygiene behaviour affects population-level disease spread — India's post-COVID context.
  • Skin microbiome: The 1.8m² skin surface has 1 million bacteria per cm². The right bacteria protect us. Over-washing, harsh soaps, and antibacterial products disrupt it.
  • Sexual hygiene: Genital hygiene basics (without shame or excess detail). Urinary tract infection (UTI) prevention — especially relevant for girls (wipe front to back, urinate after sex, stay hydrated). Testicular self-examination for boys.
  • Workplace and adult hygiene: Food safety basics (cooking temperatures, refrigeration, hand hygiene when cooking), respiratory hygiene in shared spaces, hygiene on public transport
  • Antibiotic resistance: Why finishing antibiotic courses matters. Why taking antibiotics for viral infections is harmful. India's AMR (antimicrobial resistance) crisis.

Dental & Oral Health

Systemic links, wisdom teeth, orthodontics, oral cancer awareness

tap to expand
Content
  • Oral-systemic health link: Gum disease bacteria (Porphyromonas gingivalis) have been found in atherosclerotic plaques, Alzheimer's disease brain tissue, and are linked to preterm birth and diabetes complications. Your mouth is connected to your whole body.
  • Advanced gum disease (periodontitis): Irreversible bone loss around teeth. Prevention is critical — gingivitis → periodontitis progression. Symptoms: receding gums, tooth loosening, persistent bad breath.
  • Wisdom teeth: Third molars erupt 17–25. Impaction — why some need extraction. What to expect and when to see a dentist.
  • Oral cancer awareness: India has the highest rate of oral cancer in the world — largely tobacco and gutka-related. Signs: persistent white or red patches in the mouth, ulcers not healing in 2 weeks, numbness. Early detection = high survival.
  • Tobacco and oral health: Smoking stains teeth, causes gum disease, dry socket after extractions, and is the primary cause of oral cancer. Smokeless tobacco (gutka, khaini, pan masala) equally damaging.
  • Cosmetic dentistry literacy: Teeth whitening — safe vs unsafe methods. Veneers, aligners — what they are, realistic expectations, cost awareness.
  • Dental anxiety: 36% of people avoid the dentist due to fear. Understanding procedures reduces fear. Dentists are not there to hurt you — quite the opposite.
  • Adult preventive routine: Brush 2x/day, floss 1x/day, tongue scraper, mouthwash (when appropriate), 6-monthly dental checkup, annual scaling/cleaning, pit and fissure sealants if needed.

Advanced Nutrition Science

Body composition, metabolism, eating disorders, supplements

tap to expand
Content
  • Body composition analysis: Fat mass vs fat-free mass. DEXA scan vs bioimpedance vs skinfold. Why BMI alone is inadequate. Body fat percentage ranges for health.
  • Metabolism: Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) — Harris-Benedict equation. Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE). Why crash diets lower metabolic rate. Adaptive thermogenesis.
  • Obesity mechanisms: Leptin and ghrelin — the satiety and hunger hormones. Insulin resistance — the gateway to Type 2 diabetes. Sleep and obesity link (sleep deprivation raises ghrelin). Gut microbiome and weight regulation.
  • Eating disorders: Anorexia Nervosa (restrictive), Bulimia Nervosa (binge-purge), Binge Eating Disorder, ARFID (Avoidant Restrictive Food Intake Disorder), Orthorexia. Prevalence in Indian urban adolescents rising. Recognition, destigmatisation, support pathways.
  • Supplements — evidence review:
    • Protein powder: Useful only if dietary protein is genuinely insufficient. Food-first always. Whey vs plant protein.
    • Creatine: Best-researched supplement for strength. Safe for post-puberty adolescents. Not a steroid.
    • Vitamin D: 80% of urban Indians are deficient. 1000–2000 IU/day is safe and often needed.
    • Iron: Only supplement if deficient (blood test required). Iron excess is toxic.
    • Mass gainers, pre-workouts, fat burners: Generally unnecessary, often harmful for adolescents. Understand what is in them.
  • Nutrition for cognitive performance: Omega-3 (DHA) for brain function. Breakfast and academic performance. Caffeine — effects, safe limits, dependency risk for adolescents.
  • Nutrition myths at this age: "Eating fat makes you fat." "Carbs are bad." "You need protein immediately after exercise." Evaluating nutritional claims.

Sports Science & Physical Performance

Muscle science, strength training, recovery, PEDs

tap to expand
Content
  • Muscle physiology: Sarcomeres, myosin/actin, fast-twitch vs slow-twitch fibres. How muscles grow (hypertrophy) — mechanical tension, metabolic stress, muscle damage. Supercompensation cycle.
  • Strength training science: Progressive overload principle. Rep ranges — 1-5 (strength), 6-12 (hypertrophy), 12-20 (endurance). Compound vs isolation exercises. Why form matters more than weight.
  • Cardiovascular science: VO2 max — what it is and why it matters. Heart rate zones — fat burning, aerobic, anaerobic, VO2 max zones. How to train each zone.
  • Sports injuries — recognition and management: Muscle strain vs ligament sprain vs bone stress fracture. RICE protocol (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation). When to play through pain vs when to stop. Concussion protocol.
  • Recovery science: Sleep (GH release during Stage 3 sleep). Protein timing. Active recovery. Cold therapy (ice baths) — evidence and limitations. Foam rolling.
  • Steroids and PEDs (performance-enhancing drugs): How anabolic steroids work. Short-term effects (performance). Long-term effects: liver damage, cardiovascular damage, hormonal disruption, stunted growth if used before epiphyseal closure, psychological effects (roid rage). Banned in all sports. Not worth it.
  • Designing a personal programme: 12-week personal fitness project — goal setting, periodisation, tracking, assessment. Presented as capstone project.

Sexual & Reproductive Health

Consent, STIs, healthy relationships, LGBTQ+ awareness

tap to expand
Delivery note: Class 9–10: focus on consent, relationship health, reproductive anatomy, STI awareness (conceptual). Class 11–12: deeper content including contraception awareness. Separate sessions by gender for sensitive topics. Female facilitator for girls, male for boys. All content reviewed by gynaecologist and psychologist before delivery.
Content
  • Reproductive anatomy (complete): Female — ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, vagina. Male — testes, epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, penis. Function of each.
  • Consent (advanced): Affirmative, enthusiastic, ongoing, freely given, revocable consent. Power dynamics — why consent with a teacher, employer, or someone with authority over you is inherently problematic. Age of consent in India (18). POCSO.
  • STI awareness: HIV (transmission, prevention, treatment, undetectable = untransmittable — U=U), HPV (most common STI, vaccine available — girls should receive it), Chlamydia (often asymptomatic), Herpes, Syphilis. Awareness without fear. The only comprehensive prevention is a combination of correct information + access to testing.
  • Contraception awareness (Class 11–12): Types — condoms (only method that protects against both pregnancy and STIs), oral contraceptive pills, emergency contraception, IUDs. This is awareness education, not encouragement of sexual activity.
  • LGBTQ+ inclusion: Sexual orientation and gender identity are natural variations, not choices. Section 377 decriminalisation (2018). Students may have LGBTQ+ friends, family, or identify themselves. The health impact of rejection and stigma on LGBTQ+ youth. Allyship.
  • Healthy vs unhealthy relationships: Love does not include control, jealousy, isolation, or fear. Red flags: checking your phone, controlling your friendships, making you feel guilty for spending time with family.
  • Online sexual safety: Sexting — legal risks under IT Act + POCSO even between teenagers. Non-consensual sharing of images — cybercrime. Online pornography — what it is and is not (performance, not reality, often harmful to expectations).

Advanced Menstrual Health

Cycle tracking, endometriosis, PCOS, Pee Safe advanced

tap to expand
Content — Girls
  • Cycle tracking: Apps (Flo, Clue) for tracking cycle regularity, symptoms, mood patterns. Understanding the four phases and how energy, mood, and focus change through the month — working with your cycle not against it.
  • PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome): Affects 1 in 10 Indian women. Symptoms: irregular periods, acne, excess hair growth, weight gain, difficulty conceiving. Managed (not cured) through lifestyle changes — exercise, low GI diet, stress management. Early diagnosis matters.
  • Endometriosis: Affects 1 in 10 women. Extremely underdiagnosed — average diagnosis takes 7–10 years. Severe period pain is NOT normal. Students should know the word and symptom so they can advocate for themselves.
  • Menstrual cup mastery: Full insertion and removal technique. Sterilisation between cycles. Cost-benefit analysis vs disposables. Environmental impact.
  • Period and exercise: Research shows moderate exercise improves period pain. Sports and periods — managing sport participation during menstruation. No restrictions on swimming with tampon or cup.
  • Nutrition and menstruation: Iron replenishment post-period. Magnesium for PMS (dark chocolate, leafy greens). Omega-3 for inflammation/pain reduction. Foods to limit (caffeine, excess salt worsens bloating).
  • Fertility awareness: Basic understanding of the fertile window — not for contraception (unreliable without training) but for self-knowledge and reproductive health literacy.

Mental Health — Deep Dive

Disorders, therapy, neuroscience, board exam psychology

tap to expand
Content
  • Neuroscience of mental health: The brain's key structures — prefrontal cortex (decision-making, not fully developed until age 25), amygdala (emotion processing, hyperactive in adolescence), hippocampus (memory, damaged by chronic stress). Why teenagers genuinely think and feel differently from adults.
  • Depression: Diagnostic criteria (DSM-5 simplified). Monoamine hypothesis. Why "just be positive" is harmful advice. Prevalence — 14% of Indian adolescents. Treatment: therapy + medication when appropriate + lifestyle factors.
  • Anxiety disorders: GAD, Social Anxiety, Panic Disorder, OCD, PTSD — distinctions and commonalities. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) — how it works. Exposure therapy concept.
  • Self-harm awareness: Why people self-harm (emotional regulation, not attention-seeking). What to do if a friend discloses self-harm. School referral protocol. Childline 1098.
  • Suicidal ideation: Warning signs. The importance of asking directly ("Are you thinking about suicide?"). It does not plant the idea — it opens the door. School counsellor referral immediately.
  • Seeking therapy: What therapy is (and isn't). Types — CBT, DBT, person-centred, psychodynamic. How to find a therapist. How to ask parents. iCall, Vandrevala, Nimhans helplines.
  • Medication awareness: Antidepressants — how SSRIs work (increasing serotonin availability). They are not addictive. They take 4–6 weeks to work. They do not change your personality. They are tools, not crutches.
  • Board exam mental health: Cortisol curve and optimal stress for performance. Cognitive load management. Identity beyond grades — who you are is not your percentile.
  • Indian mental health context: Cultural stigma, family pressure, career-only focus, dismissal of emotional needs. How to navigate this and still get the support you need.

Relationships & Emotional Bonds

Attachment, romantic relationships, heartbreak, family conflict

tap to expand
Content
  • Attachment theory: Bowlby and Ainsworth — secure, anxious-preoccupied, dismissive-avoidant, fearful-avoidant attachment styles. How your early relationships shape who you are drawn to and how you behave in relationships.
  • Relationship anxiety: Hypervigilance to partner's behaviour. Fear of abandonment. Over-analysing messages. Jealousy. Root causes and management.
  • Romantic relationships at this age: What is developmentally normal. Healthy expectations — companionship, shared interests, mutual respect — not completion, possession, or validation of worth.
  • Love languages: Words of affirmation, acts of service, receiving gifts, quality time, physical touch (Chapman). Understanding your own and others' languages reduces conflict.
  • Breakups and heartbreak: The neuroscience of heartbreak — brain processes romantic rejection similarly to physical pain (fMRI research). It is real. Time heals — but the grieving process is valid. What healthy recovery looks like.
  • Toxic relationship patterns: Gaslighting, love bombing, isolation, control, emotional abuse. These can happen in any relationship — friend, romantic, family. They are not love.
  • Parent-teen conflict: The developmental purpose of separating from parents — necessary for identity formation. How to communicate needs to parents in ways that work. The difference between healthy independence and recklessness.
  • Loneliness vs aloneness: Loneliness (painful isolation) vs solitude (chosen restorative aloneness). The modern epidemic of loneliness in digitally connected generations. Building real social connection.

Digital Wellness & Tech Literacy

Algorithm design, addiction, digital identity, AI and health

tap to expand
Content
  • How social media algorithms work: Engagement maximisation — the algorithm shows you content that makes you feel strong emotions (outrage, envy, lust, fear) because those create longer sessions. Understanding this makes you a smarter user.

Financial Health

Banking, insurance, compound interest, health economics, budgeting

tap to expand
Content
  • Financial stress and physical health: Chronic financial stress elevates cortisol → hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular disease. People in debt have measurably worse immune function. Financial health IS health.
  • Compound interest (deep): The eighth wonder of the world. ₹5,000/month at 12% from age 18 = ₹4.7 crore at 60. The same invested from age 30 = ₹1.5 crore. Starting 12 years earlier = 3x the wealth. This one concept, internalised at 16, changes lives.
  • Banking products: Savings account, FD, RD, PPF, NPS — what each is, who it's for, expected returns. The difference between saving and investing.
  • Mutual funds and SIP basics: What a mutual fund is. What SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) means. Index funds — the most evidence-based investment for most people. Do not speculate; invest.
  • Health insurance: Why it is essential. Premium vs sum insured. Cashless hospitalisation. Pre-existing disease clauses. Family floater vs individual. PMJAY (Ayushman Bharat) — government health cover and who is eligible.
  • Budget and cash flow: Monthly income vs expenses tracking. 50/30/20 rule — needs, wants, savings. Zero-based budgeting. Emergency fund — 6 months of expenses.
  • Debt literacy: Good debt (education loan, home loan — asset-building) vs bad debt (credit card revolving balance — wealth-destroying). EMI trap. The psychological effects of debt on mental health.
  • UPI, fintech, and digital safety: QR code fraud, phishing, vishing. Two-factor authentication. Never share OTP. Understanding digital transaction trails.
  • Career and income: Understanding that the choice of career affects lifelong financial health — not just salary, but stability, growth, healthcare benefits, work stress. Choosing work you can sustain, not just the highest package.

Career Wellness & Life Skills

Environmental & Planetary Health

Climate and health, toxins, urban health, eco-anxiety

tap to expand
Content
  • Climate change and health: Heat waves (India 2024 — 300+ deaths), air quality degradation, water scarcity, vector-borne disease expansion (malaria, dengue moving north). Climate change is a health crisis.

Ethics, leadership, giving back, health equity
